# INT-07 — Zoe

**M:** Hi, thanks for making time. Before we start — is it okay if I record?

**Zoe (P):** Sure, no problem.

**M:** Great. Let's start with something simple — how long have you been using Spotify?

**P:** Ooh, a long time. Maybe eight years? Could be nine. I still remember the version with the monthly playback limit, do you even remember that? That gray interface.

**M:** I do, yeah. How would you describe your relationship with music in general — not just the app?

<!-- anchor: bio-relationship -->
**P:** It's a big part of my life, honestly. I've been collecting vinyl for about four years, I've probably got around a hundred and fifty records by now. I go to concerts, mostly niche electronic stuff, sometimes post-punk. I kind of treat Spotify as... I don't know, like a library for browsing before I buy? If I like something, I check whether it's out on vinyl and then I buy it.

**M:** So Spotify functions as discovery, and vinyl is the final form of ownership.

**P:** Exactly, well put. Though, of course, ninety percent of the time I'm still listening through Spotify day-to-day, because I don't always have a turntable handy.

---

<!-- anchor: usage-context -->
**M:** Tell me what a typical day with Spotify looks like for you. When do you usually put it on?

**P:** Hmm, in the morning while I'm getting ready, it's usually something more upbeat. At work — I work as a graphic designer, so I sit at a computer a lot — it depends on the task, honestly. If I'm doing something creative, a brief, research, I prefer something without vocals, some ambient or instrumental stuff, so the words don't get in the way of my thinking. But if I'm doing something mechanical, I don't know, exporting files, revisions, I can listen to pretty much anything.

**M:** And in the evening?

**P:** Evening is my time for discovery. I sit down, sometimes with records next to me, and I actually explore. That's probably my favorite moment of the week, honestly — Friday evening, new releases.

**M:** I see. And devices — what do you usually listen on?

**P:** Phone on the go, laptop at work, and I've got a Bluetooth speaker at home, a small one, sits on a shelf. And headphones, of course — at work I use headphones so I don't bother anyone else.

---

<!-- anchor: library-organization -->
**M:** How do you manage your library? Do you have a system?

**P:** Oh, that I actually have very organized, maybe even too organized. *(laughs)* I have separate playlists by genre, so for example I've got "ambient for work," I've got "techno for running," I've got "new stuff to check out" — that's a kind of working playlist where I dump everything that catches my interest from Release Radar or Discover Weekly, and then once a week, usually on Sunday, I listen through it again calmly and decide what stays and what goes.

**M:** That sounds like a fairly involved process.

**P:** It is, but it's enjoyable for me, it's not a chore. I've also got archival playlists, chronological ones, "Zoe 2022," "Zoe 2023" and so on — like my personal yearbooks.

**M:** What about Liked Songs? Do you use that?

**P:** Almost never, honestly. That's like a junk drawer for me — if I need to click something quickly, on the fly, and don't have time to assign it to a specific playlist, it goes there. But then I never go back through it, because it's already chaos in there.

---

<!-- anchor: algorithm-drift-mainstream -->
**M:** You mentioned Discover Weekly. How would you rate the accuracy of those recommendations?

**P:** That's an interesting topic, because I have mixed feelings about it. It used to be really good, I felt like it understood my taste. But I've noticed a pattern — if I listen to something more mainstream, say because a friend sent me some hit song and I wanted to check it out, then for the next week or two my entire Discover Weekly drifts toward more pop, obvious stuff. Like a single play can outweigh hundreds of hours of listening to niche electronic music.

**M:** Does that happen often?

**P:** More often than I'd like. And it's frustrating, because that's exactly why I pay for Premium, to get good recommendations in my niche, not things I'd hear on the radio or on Instagram anyway.

**M:** Do you do anything to fix it?

**P:** I've tried removing things from my history, but that doesn't always work. Sometimes I just ignore Discover Weekly for two or three weeks until it "goes back to normal" on its own. It's a bit absurd that I have to wait for the algorithm to forget.

---

<!-- anchor: catalog-removal -->
**M:** Has it ever happened that an album or a track disappeared from Spotify?

**P:** Oh my god, yes, and that probably annoys me the most out of everything. I had a situation once where an entire playlist I'd been building for maybe six months lost five or six tracks overnight — they just vanished, no warning. It turned out to be some licensing issue, the artist had some dispute with the label or something like that, I don't remember exactly.

**M:** What did you do then?

**P:** I had to manually search for where those tracks still existed, whether they were on Bandcamp, whether I could buy the mp3 somewhere else. That's exactly the moment when I think to myself, "this is why I buy vinyl" — because nobody's taking a record off my shelf. But yeah, it's annoying, because I waste time rebuilding something I already had organized.

**M:** Did Spotify notify you about it in any way?

**P:** No, absolutely nothing. I noticed myself, because the playlist suddenly had a different length than I remembered. There was no notification, no email, nothing.

---

<!-- anchor: gapless-playback -->
**M:** You mentioned evening listening, exploring, earlier. What about when you listen to longer, continuous things, like DJ mixes or concept albums?

**P:** That's another thing that irritates me. Crossfade and those automatic transitions between tracks sometimes really ruin the whole experience, especially with albums that are meant to be one continuous whole — a lot of electronic music is like that, where tracks flow into each other, and if Spotify throws its own fade in there, it kills the artist's whole intent. I had to learn to turn off crossfade manually in settings, but even then sometimes it resets after an update and I have to remember to check.

**M:** Have you tried other apps for that kind of listening?

**P:** I had a phase with a local player, for FLAC files, when I bought stuff directly from artists. But that's extra hassle, moving files around, so I mostly just stay on Spotify and accept that sometimes it's not perfect.

---

<!-- anchor: social-features -->
**M:** Let's move to something else — what do you think about the social features, like Wrapped or Blend?

**P:** Wrapped is... *(laughs)* ...it's fun, but I have kind of a weird feeling about it. Because on one hand it's nice to see a summary, but on the other hand I know everyone posts it on Instagram, and I kind of unconsciously start comparing my stats to other people's. "Oh, this person listened to more minutes than me" or something like "my top artist isn't original enough." It's dumb, I know, but that's how I am.

**M:** What about Blend?

**P:** I did Blend once with my boyfriend, as a joke. We ended up with this weird playlist that was half his rap, half my ambient, jumping back and forth without any sense. Fun little curiosity, but not something I'd use regularly.

---

<!-- anchor: pricing -->
**M:** How do you feel about the price of Premium relative to what you actually use?

**P:** I pay and I don't complain, honestly. I don't listen to podcasts, audiobooks don't interest me, but the sound quality itself and the lack of ads are worth the price to me. Maybe if they raised it again I'd think twice, but for now I don't have an issue with it.

**M:** Have you ever considered any of the competing services?

**P:** I looked into Bandcamp as a platform, but that's a different model, you buy directly there, it's not streaming in that sense. Among the typical streaming services I'm staying with Spotify, because that's where my whole history is, all my playlists, and moving that elsewhere would be a nightmare.

---

**M:** We're wrapping up. Is there anything I didn't ask that you'd like to mention?

**P:** I think just that the most frustrating thing for me is the feeling that the deeper I get into a niche, the less Spotify is able to help me, and that's exactly the moment when good recommendations would be most valuable. Like the product was designed for someone who listens to the mainstream, and I'm there kind of by accident.

**M:** Thank you so much for the conversation.

**P:** No problem, I enjoyed talking.
