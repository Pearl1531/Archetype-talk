# Typing while driving is dangerous

## Content

NHTSA (the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration) documents texting/typing as the most dangerous form of distracted driving, since it combines visual, manual, and cognitive distraction at once — reading or sending a text takes a driver's eyes off the road for about 5 seconds, which at 55 mph is roughly the length of a football field driven blind. Aggregated safety research (cited via The Zebra and Forbes Advisor) puts the crash-risk increase at up to 23× for texting drivers and 3.6× for handheld phone use generally, compared to undistracted driving.

## Takeaways

- Typing on a phone while driving isn't a minor inconvenience — it's a well-documented, severe safety risk, independent of any individual driver's typing speed or accuracy.
- For a driver who is already slower/less accurate at typing (e.g. due to dyslexia), the same few seconds of required attention are stretched longer, compounding the risk rather than just adding to it.
