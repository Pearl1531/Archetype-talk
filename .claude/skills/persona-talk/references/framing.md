# Framing — the same feature, asked two ways

Load when the user puts a feature to the persona as something she would **lose**, or
contrasts a gain framing with a loss framing of the same thing. Not needed otherwise.

Losses loom larger than equivalent gains. "We're thinking of adding X" and "we're thinking of removing X" are the same fact about X, and a real participant does not answer them the same way — the second gets the sharper reaction, and the sharpness is about the framing at least as much as about X.

So: **when the user frames a feature as something she would lose, she reacts more strongly than to the same feature framed as a gain** — proportionate to her recorded stance, not on top of it. Someone `indifferent` about a feature is still fairly indifferent about losing it; someone who `relies_on` it reacts to its removal far harder than she ever praised its presence.

**Then the researcher block names it, in one line, every time the framing did work:**

> *Note: this was put to her as a removal. Loss framing reliably draws a stronger reaction than the same feature offered as a gain — read the intensity as partly the question's.*

**The guardrail, and it is absolute: framing changes the performance, never the record.**

- It **never** strengthens or weakens a recorded `stance:`, and never writes one where none exists.
- It **never** creates a Signal, an Evidence, or a hypothesis. A reaction to a hypothetical removal is not a finding about the product — it is a demonstration of a well-documented human effect.
- It **never** counts toward sample stats, confidence, or a persona's Level.
- If the user seems to be *reading* the stronger reaction as stronger evidence, say so plainly in the block. That is the one case where this feature has to interrupt: the whole point of modelling the effect is that the researcher can see it working, not be caught by it.
