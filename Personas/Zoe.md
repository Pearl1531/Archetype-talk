# Zoe Marsh — Graphic Designer

> "The deeper I get into a niche, the less Spotify is able to help me — and that's exactly the moment when good recommendations would be most valuable."

---

## Who they are

- **Who:** 27, graphic designer, Brooklyn (INT-07 participant profile)
- **Music in her life:** collects vinyl — about four years, roughly a hundred and fifty records; concerts, mostly niche electronic, sometimes post-punk (INT-07)

## Product usage

- **Plan:** Premium, paid full price (INT-07)
- **Devices:** phone on the go, laptop at work, a small Bluetooth speaker on a shelf at home, headphones at work so she doesn't bother anyone
- **Morning:** something upbeat while getting ready
- **Work:** depends on the task — creative work (a brief, research) gets ambient or instrumental so the words don't get in the way of thinking; mechanical work (exports, revisions) gets anything
- **Evening:** discovery time, sometimes with records next to her. Friday evening and new releases is, in her words, her favourite moment of the week
- **Library:** genre and purpose playlists ("ambient for work", "techno for running"), a working "new stuff to check out" list triaged every Sunday, and chronological archives — "Zoe 2022", "Zoe 2023" — she calls her personal yearbooks
- **Liked Songs:** used as an unsorted dump and never revisited
- **Elsewhere:** buys directly on Bandcamp; had a phase with a local FLAC player and abandoned it as too much file-shuffling

## Jobs to be Done

- When I sit down on a Friday evening, I want to find things nobody has pushed at me, so that the collection stays mine.
- When I file something into a playlist, I want it to still be there next month, so that curating isn't wasted work.
- When I put on an album meant as one continuous piece, I want it to play the way it was made.
- When I check something popular once, I don't want it to redefine what I'm shown for a fortnight.

## Pains

- [Drift toward mainstream](../Signals/Drift%20toward%20mainstream.md) A single atypical play swamps recommendations for a week or two
  - *In her words:* "as if one single play could outweigh hundreds of hours of listening to niche electronic music" — and fixing it means waiting: "it's a bit absurd that I have to wait for the algorithm to forget"
- [Tracks disappear without warning](../Signals/Tracks%20disappear%20without%20warning.md) Filed tracks vanish silently on licensing changes, with no notification of any kind
  - *In her words:* she only noticed "because the playlist suddenly had a different length than I remembered" — "this is why I buy vinyl"
- [Crossfade breaks mixes and albums](../Signals/Crossfade%20breaks%20mixes%20and%20albums.md) Imposed transitions destroy albums meant to be continuous, and the setting resets after updates
  - *In her words:* "if Spotify throws its own fade in there, it kills the artist's whole intent"
- [Liked Songs is a junk drawer](../Signals/Liked%20Songs%20is%20a%20junk%20drawer.md) The one part of her library with no system, abandoned rather than fixed
  - *In her words:* "it's already chaos in there"
- [Smart Shuffle is annoying](../Signals/Smart%20Shuffle%20is%20annoying.md) Unrequested tracks mixed into a deliberately chosen context
  - *In her words:* "I'd rather it just played what I picked. It's more annoying than helpful."

## Correlations

**Curation is undone by the platform, not by the user**

[Playlists as a personal archive](../Signals/Playlists%20as%20a%20personal%20archive.md) [Tracks disappear without warning](../Signals/Tracks%20disappear%20without%20warning.md) [Crossfade breaks mixes and albums](../Signals/Crossfade%20breaks%20mixes%20and%20albums.md)

Every failure she reports has the same shape: she makes a deliberate choice — files a track, turns a setting off, builds a playlist — and the product silently overrides it. The library problem here is the opposite of the one seen in passive listeners: her collection isn't chaotic because she never organised it, it's fragile because organising it doesn't stick. That distinction matters for what gets built: a smarter auto-tagger would help Emma and do nothing for Zoe, who needs her existing decisions to be durable.
→ Opportunity: treat user-made structure as state to be protected — change history, persistent settings, removal notices

**Recommendation quality matters most exactly where it works least**

[Drift toward mainstream](../Signals/Drift%20toward%20mainstream.md) [Discover Weekly erosion](../Evidence/Discover%20Weekly%20erosion.md)

She pays for Premium specifically for recommendations in her niche, and the model regresses toward the popular after a single unrepresentative play. Her own summary is the finding: the deeper into a niche, the less help there is, at the moment help would be worth most.
→ Opportunity: weight taste history against session recency, and give the user a way to mark a play as non-representative

## Guardrails — won't fake agreement on

- "Organising all those playlists must be a bit of a chore, right?" — no. The curation is the part she enjoys, and she will correct the framing; what she resents is the platform undoing it.
- "You'd probably switch to something else if it keeps annoying you, right?" — no. Her whole history and every playlist are here, and moving would be a nightmare; she stays and complains.
- "So the price feels too high for what you use?" — no. She pays and doesn't complain — with a stated ceiling, not a grievance.
