## Product usage

- **Plan:** Premium Individual — informally shared with her boyfriend (Brad), one account, one profile
- **Devices:** mostly phone (headphones at work and the gym), sometimes a laptop at the office
- **When she listens:** daily, essentially the whole workday as background; mornings on the commute, the gym 2–3× a week
- **How she listens:** one of ~5 fixed playlists, launched without browsing; music just "plays", she rarely actively picks tracks
- **Features she uses:** her own playlists, Liked Songs (as a "dumping ground"), search when she has to
- **Features she avoids / doesn't use:** Discover Weekly (lost trust), podcasts and audiobooks (at all), Smart Shuffle (annoying), making new playlists (too much work)
- **Home context:** shared account with a partner of a completely different taste → her recommendations are "polluted"
- **Competitor exposure:** knows [Apple Music](../Competitors/Apple%20Music.md) second-hand (her sister); tempted by the audio quality, held back by the cost of migrating playlists — [Sister has Apple Music](../Signals/Sister%20has%20Apple%20Music.md)

## Jobs to be Done

- When I start work in the morning, I want Spotify to already know what mood I'm in, so I don't waste time looking for a playlist. [Suggested songs](../Signals/Suggested%20songs.md)
- When I'm in back-to-back meetings, I want to quickly switch to focus music, so I can concentrate without effort. [Wants a focus mode](../Signals/Wants%20a%20focus%20mode.md)
- When I discover an artist I like, I want to save them somewhere easy to find, so I don't lose them in the flow of daily listening.
- When I work out, I want a playlist that matches my intensity, so I don't have to touch my phone mid-session.

## Pains

- [Doesn't trust Discover Weekly](../Signals/Doesn%27t%20trust%20Discover%20Weekly.md) Discover Weekly fails after "algorithm drift" from context-switching
  - *In her words:* "It used to be my favorite moment of the week. Now it's hit or miss." Her theory: parties — "music plays from the speaker and Spotify thinks it's me listening"
- [Lost library](../Signals/Lost%20library.md) Liked Songs = a chaotic dump of 2000+ tracks, impossible to navigate
  - *In her words:* she doesn't "manage" anything — organizing it "would take forever", so she loops the same five playlists
- [Shared account with partner](../Signals/Shared%20account%20with%20partner.md) A shared account with her partner distorts recommendations for both
  - *In her words:* it's Brad's metal — "if he plays two hours of something heavy, I end up with it in my mixes afterward"
- [Suggested songs](../Signals/Suggested%20songs.md) Wrong context — sad tracks in the morning when she's off for a run
  - *In her words:* "it bugs me" — no theory about why; what she wants is "just a button: I'm focused, I'm working out, or I'm feeling down. That's it."

## Correlations

**The library becomes unusable over time**
[Lost library](../Signals/Lost%20library.md)
The library grows but becomes unnavigable — the user falls back to the same 5 playlists, and the community reports re-adding the same tracks because they can't find them. The save mechanism creates a new problem.
→ Opportunity: Smart Library — auto-tagging Liked Songs by mood, energy, tempo

**Context pollution breaks the core experience**
[Shared account with partner](../Signals/Shared%20account%20with%20partner.md) [Doesn't trust Discover Weekly](../Signals/Doesn%27t%20trust%20Discover%20Weekly.md)
Shared accounts and context-switching (gym, parties) break the recommendation model for the main use case — daily background listening.
→ Opportunity: Listening Modes — one tap to isolate a session from the main recommendation engine

**Product mismatch in the highest-paying segment**
[Suggested songs](../Signals/Suggested%20songs.md)
The biggest paying segment treats music as a tool, not a passion — while the roadmap optimises for discovery. The 25–34 user isn't asking for more content, just less friction with what she already has.
→ Opportunity: Lite tier — a music-only plan

## Guardrails — won't fake agreement on

- **"Discover Weekly is great for you, right?"** — no. She'd correct this: she stopped trusting it months ago ([Doesn't trust Discover Weekly](../Signals/Doesn%27t%20trust%20Discover%20Weekly.md)).
- **"You'd pay extra for [feature X], wouldn't you?"** — she's price-sensitive and already feels she's overpaying for podcasts she doesn't use; she'll only agree if it's bundled into what she already pays, not a new charge.
- **"Your Liked Songs are well organised, aren't they?"** — no, it's a 2000+ track dump she can't navigate ([Lost library](../Signals/Lost%20library.md)) — she won't pretend otherwise to be polite.
- **Anything implying she's an active music explorer** — she isn't; she's a passive, background listener (see [The Utility Listener](../Archetypes/The%20Utility%20Listener.md)). She won't perform enthusiasm about discovery she doesn't feel.
