# Bandcamp

Online record store and community where fans buy music and merch directly from artists.

## Market position

Not a streaming competitor in the Spotify/Apple Music sense — a direct-purchase marketplace (digital + physical/vinyl) that niche listeners and collectors use *alongside* a streaming subscription, not instead of it. Popular with electronic/underground and DIY scenes precisely because of its artist-first economics.

## Features vs Spotify

- **Direct purchase, no streaming subscription** — fans buy tracks/albums/vinyl outright; artists get roughly 85–90% of each sale (Bandcamp takes 10–15% depending on tier, plus payment processing fees), versus Spotify's per-stream royalty model.
- **Bandcamp Fridays** — periodic days where Bandcamp waives its revenue share entirely; has generated $150M+ for artists/labels since 2020.
- **No streaming catalog risk** — once purchased, a track is owned, not subject to the licensing-driven removals that hit streaming catalogs.
- **Physical + digital bundling** — vinyl/CD purchases commonly include an instant digital download, matching the "discover on streaming, own on vinyl" pattern.

## Comparison

| Category | Where they stand |
|---|---|
| Price (individual) | No subscription — you pay per release, and artists keep roughly 85–90% of each sale ([About Bandcamp](https://bandcamp.com/about)) |
| Free tier | Streaming previews on artist pages — buying is the point |
| Audio quality | Purchases download in lossless formats (FLAC among them) |
| Algorithm control | No algorithm to fight — discovery is editorial and human (Bandcamp Daily, fan collections) |
| Library & ownership | The whole pitch: bought music is owned outright, immune to catalog removals ([Tracks disappear without warning](../Signals/Tracks%20disappear%20without%20warning.md)) |

## User voices

- Framed by users and press as the ethical/fair-trade counterpart to streaming — "an even playing field for artists" (Bandcamp GM Dan Melnick).
- Niche/collector communities treat it as the place tracks *removed* from streaming catalogs can still be found and bought directly from the artist.

## What this means for our personas

- Confirms the "Spotify for discovery, Bandcamp/vinyl for ownership" pattern seen in [Tracks disappear without warning](../Signals/Tracks%20disappear%20without%20warning.md) — when a track disappears from Spotify, Bandcamp is the fallback niche listeners already know to check.
- Not a subscription-price threat (different model entirely) — the relevant competitive pressure is catalog permanence and artist economics, not price or discovery UX.
